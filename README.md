## How to check an offer before buying

A cashback percentage by itself is not enough to decide whether an offer is useful. Before clicking through, check the following information on the store page.

### 1. The reward type

Cashback may be displayed as:

- A percentage of eligible spending
- A fixed dollar amount
- A maximum reward
- A first-order bonus
- A limited-time boosted rate
- A travel or booking reward confirmed after the trip

A 10% rate may sound better than a $5 fixed reward, but the percentage may apply only to selected categories or exclude shipping and taxes. ShopBack explains that its calculations are based on the displayed store rate and that many merchants exclude shipping fees and taxes from the cashback calculation.

### 2. Product and category exclusions

Retailers often exclude gift cards, marketplace sellers, subscriptions, warranties, prescription items, or other special categories. These exclusions can vary from one store to another.

Do not assume that every item in a retailer’s catalog qualifies simply because the store has a cashback listing.

### 3. Coupon compatibility

A merchant’s own sale price may be compatible with cashback, while an outside coupon code may not be. Some offers allow only promotional codes shown on the ShopBack store page.

The safest approach is to check the coupon terms before copying a code from another website. If the code is not listed as eligible, it may reduce or cancel the reward.

### 4. New-customer requirements

Some offers apply only to first-time customers. Others distinguish between new and existing customers or place limits on the number of eligible transactions.

ShopBack’s terms explain that cashback eligibility can depend on campaigns, promotions, discount codes, and different user tiers.

### 5. Confirmation timing

A shopping order may be tracked quickly but confirmed much later. Travel bookings can be especially slow because the merchant may wait until the hotel stay, flight, or rental period is completed before confirming the commission.

A cashback monitor is most useful when it separates tracked, pending, and confirmed rewards rather than treating every recorded click as guaranteed money.

## What can cause cashback to disappear?

Cashback is not normally removed at random. The most common causes are connected to order changes, tracking, or eligibility.

### Returns and cancellations

If an order is returned, canceled, exchanged, or partially refunded, the merchant may reduce or remove the associated cashback. That is expected because the merchant’s commission may also be reversed.

### Outside coupon codes

A code from a third-party coupon website may conflict with the cashback offer. Some stores allow outside codes, but others do not.

### Multiple attribution sources

If you click through another advertising link, coupon site, or cashback service after activating ShopBack, the last tracking source may receive credit for the order. That can prevent ShopBack from confirming the cashback.

### Device and app switching

Starting on a desktop and finishing in a mobile app can interrupt the original tracking session. The same issue can happen when opening a new private window, changing browsers, or using a different account at checkout.

### Ineligible products

A qualifying store does not mean every product qualifies. Carefully check exclusions for gift cards, taxes, shipping, subscriptions, and marketplace items.

### Travel timing

Travel cashback may remain pending until after the stay or trip has ended. A booking confirmation alone may not be enough for the merchant to approve the reward.

These limitations are why a cashback monitor should be treated as a way to check eligibility and track progress, not as an automatic discount applied to every order.

A coupon site usually answers one question: “Is there a code I can use?”

| Question | Coupon site | Cashback monitor |
